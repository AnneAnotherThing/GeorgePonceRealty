The flag

Taking care of it

The flag I bring to closing is meant to fly, not sit in a closet. A few things worth knowing:

  • Placement. By the front door at an angle, or on a porch-mounted pole. If we didn't find the spot together on closing day, send me a photo of the front of the house and I'll tell you where I'd put it.
  • Weather. Arizona sun is harder on a flag than rain. Bringing it in during monsoon season and the worst of summer will double its life.
  • Wear. When it fades or frays, that flag has served with honor. Retire it respectfully; many VFW and American Legion posts accept flags for proper retirement.
  • Replacement. And then call me. The replacement is on me. Any client, any year, no questions.

George's homeowner tips

Little moves that pay for themselves

Things I tell my clients after closing. Filter by what you care about, tap to open, and steal freely.

Set the water heater to 120°FSave money

Most come from the factory hotter than you need. 120°F cuts the bill, slows mineral buildup, and prevents scald burns for kids and grandparents.

Learn more at energy.gov →

Ask your utility about rebatesSave money

APS and SRP both pay you to make the house efficient: shade screens, smart thermostats, AC tune-ups, insulation. Most homeowners never claim a dime of it.

APS programs →   SRP rebates →

Sun-block the west windowsSave money

Shade screens or solar film on west-facing glass is the cheapest cooling upgrade in Arizona. The AC fights the afternoon sun less, and the carpet stops fading.

Window coverings guide →

Test GFCI outlets twice a yearSafety

The outlets with the little buttons, in kitchens, baths and outside. Press TEST, then RESET. If it won't trip or reset, replace it; that's the outlet that saves lives around water.

How GFCIs protect you →

Know your two shutoffsSafety

Water main and gas meter. Find them the week you move in, tag them, and show everyone in the house. A burst line at 2am is not the moment to go looking.

Home safety skills at ready.gov →

Water trees deeply, not dailyDesert life

Desert trees want a long, slow soak every week or two, not a daily sprinkle. Deep roots survive July; shallow ones don't. Your water bill drops too.

Landscape watering guides →

Monsoon-proof the patioDesert life

June through September, anything light on the patio is a projectile waiting for a microburst. Umbrellas down after use, cushions in a box, trampoline anchored.

Arizona monsoon awareness →

Walk the roof line every springDesert life

From the ground, with your phone camera zoomed. You're looking for slipped tiles, exposed underlayment, and debris in valleys. Five minutes now beats finding it during the first monsoon.

Keep a home file from day oneProtect your value

One folder: closing docs, receipts for every improvement, warranties, paint colors, contractor names. When you sell, documented upgrades are worth real money, and until then you'll never re-buy the wrong paint.

Photograph the house once a yearProtect your value

Walk every room and the yard with your phone each January. If a storm, fire or break-in ever happens, a dated photo inventory turns an insurance fight into a checklist.

Home inventory how-to →

Don't skip the yearly termite lookProtect your value

Arizona is termite country, period. A yearly inspection runs cheap; mud tubes caught early are a minor treatment instead of structural repair at resale.
